
Long ago, in an enchanted forest, there lived a King who spent his days locked inside a castle of his own making. The castle walls were carefully crafted from stones fused together by the maw of cold logic. Within those walls the King would pace endlessly through corridors, lost in thought, wrapped in grand robes of thick fur and bearing a bejewelled crown that pressed heavily upon his brow.

Outside the fortress roamed a free-spirited Sprite that delighted in all the forest’s smallest wonders—stopping to watch a caterpillar on a furious quest for aphids and marvelling at the scent of the euphorynths (flowers with pollen grains that causes anyone who sniff it to smile). One day, the Sprite noticed the solemn castle rising above the forest. Curious about its occupant she crossed the ox-bow moat and entered the castle she found the King sitting alone.

“Why do you hide behind these walls?” she asked gently. “Outside, there’s a vast forests bulging with life — pines with hollow trunks that echo the wind as if the forest itself were speaking, mushrooms whose spores taste like peanut butter and skies inked in teary blue that stretch above mountains made entirely of glass. The King frowned. “I have everything I need right here. Venturing outside would be foolish,” he said sullenly. “Besides, someone must keep the castle tidy, or it will reflect poorly on me as a ruler.”

Still, each day the Sprite returned to the castle and with each visit the King grew increasingly intrigued by the tales from the forest. One day, she spoke of a valley of Moonblossoms –– flowers that bud only when the moon sets, and a beach inhabited by red spiders that stain the sand with a purple hue. Another day she recounted a bottomless lake filled with terrifying tooth-studded creatures that rise slowly from the depths at the rate a feather falls. Each time, the King listened with rapturous curiosity, but the thought of leaving his fortress filled him with unease. “Outside lies uncertainty, and uncertainty invites chaos,” he thought to himself.  Yet each night the King had vivid dreams where he sat naked beneath towering sunflowers and shared strange foods with the sprite. In his dreams he felt light and unburdened as their bare feet glided silently together across the forest floor, and although he had never seen the ocean he could hear the gentle sound of waves breaking.

One day, tucking his robe tightly around himself and adjusting his crown the King nervously announced, “Very well, I would like to see this magical forest,” but cautioned the Sprite not to wander too far, insisting they return by dusk. As they ventured into the dense forest thickets the air grew thick and heavy, clinging to the King’s skin as beads of sweat gathered on his brow. His fur robe, made for cold stone halls, became stifling in the heat, and the crown burrowed painfully into his forehead. The Sprite, eager to share the wonders of the forest, pointed excitedly to a vine of glimmergrapes –– fruits that burst into tiny fireworks when squeezed –– and exclaimed gleefully when her taste buds began to pulse rhythmically with sourness. “Brine-berries! Although they’re not actually berries" , she added. "They’re magnetic fireflies, each flicker sends a flavour wave across your tongue!” But the King’s face had clouded over with the incomprehensible vastness of the outside world. “Take me back,” he insisted. And so, they returned to his skull-sized kingdom.

Although the King did not relinquish his castle overnight a new spirit had been ignited in him. Day by day, the crown and robe stayed behind a little longer, and each journey beyond the gates carried him into deeper waters where he began to discover himself. One night, laying together beneath the night sky his mind unwound from the axel of discursive thought and the Milky Way seemed to pour down inside each breath with a thunderous roar. As he closed his eyes the forest hummed with infinite possibilities and for the first time he smiled a genuine, unguarded smile.
