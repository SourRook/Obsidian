It can direct the attention of the elephant by prodding it, but at the end of the day, the elephant can do as it pleases. This monkey is often in for a big disappointment when it notices that it makes decisions, but the elephant is not reacting to them. Sometimes I feel the cause of all my suffering arisesg in the mind of the frustrated monkey that thinks it is in charge

The elephant part of the mind is much older, especially with respect to the high-level concepts that the monkey is developing to analyse its own behaviour. The attentional control system is old, but the analytical system that the monkey employs is rather young. Feelings are a way for the emotions and motivational impulses of the elephant to be accessible to the analytic monkey. So in this sense, we could argue that there is a lot of stuff that precedes the feelings, because it is a way of communicating between subsystems in the brain.

It's the perceptual system that does the majority of operations — something like a distributed neural network with continuous dynamics — and this analytical, grammatical engine. How do we interface these two systems? We can take these features that are being computed in this distributed perceptual architecture and translate them to the localised, discrete analytical model. We do this by projecting into a space — and the only space that is consistently available is the body map. So feelings are projected to the body map to disambiguate them.
